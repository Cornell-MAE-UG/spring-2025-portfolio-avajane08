This assignment focused on applying heat transfer principles to analyze real engineering systems using thermal resistance networks and conduction–convection models. In the first problem, I learned how to construct a thermal resistance network to represent heat transfer through a photovoltaic (PV) panel, including conduction through different material layers and convection and radiation to the surroundings. This process required identifying the relevant thermal resistances, making reasonable assumptions, and calculating surface temperatures and efficiency. In the second problem, I analyzed heat transfer in a cylindrical rod exposed to convection, deriving a relationship between geometric parameters and surface temperature. This reinforced how conduction and convection interact in extended surfaces and how analytical expressions can be used to evaluate design constraints.

These concepts are useful for future engineering work because many thermal systems can be simplified into resistance networks or similar analytical models. Being able to quickly estimate temperatures, heat fluxes, and system performance is important when designing components such as solar panels, heat exchangers, or electronic cooling systems. The assignment also emphasized how geometry, material properties, and environmental conditions influence thermal performance. Developing these modeling skills will be valuable in mechanical engineering applications where predicting and controlling heat transfer is critical for efficiency, safety, and reliability.
