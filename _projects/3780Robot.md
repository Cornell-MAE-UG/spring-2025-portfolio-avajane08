In this project, my team and I designed and built a competitive block-collecting robot for MAE 3780 that focused on simplicity, reliability, and effective block collection. I contributed heavily to the robot’s mechanical design, including prototyping and refining the L-shaped collection arm, organizing materials and integration logistics, and helping develop the overall competition strategy. Our robot used a laser-cut acrylic chassis, two wheel-driving servos, and a rotating L-arm made from tongue depressors to collect and protect blocks while following a spiral movement algorithm. Throughout the design process, we adapted our ideas based on testing and milestone feedback, troubleshooting issues involving sensors, battery voltage, traction, and arm mounting. During the competition, the robot performed well when unobstructed and successfully collected blocks, but collisions with other robots revealed weaknesses in durability, maneuverability, and recovery from entanglement. Reflecting on the project, I learned the importance of designing for real competition conditions, using sturdier materials, and balancing simplicity with functionality while working collaboratively to solve mechanical and programming challenges.
