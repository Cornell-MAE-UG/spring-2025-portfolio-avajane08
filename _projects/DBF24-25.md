### Landing Gear Test Rig

<figure class="side-image">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/dbf-landing-gear-test.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Testing the landing gear with a digital hanging scale while measuring displacement with a tape measure">
</figure>

During the first semester of the 24-25 season, I designed and built a wooden test rig for the plane's front landing gear. The goal was to determine how much load the gear could tolerate. To run the test, I used a digital hanging scale to pull down on the gear. I recorded the scale reading along with how far the top of the landing gear was displaced at each point. From that data I estimated the maximum load that can be applied to the front landing gear.

This was my first real project and contribution to DBF.

### Fuselage Fabrication

<figure class="side-image">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/dbf-vacuum-bagging.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="A part sealed in a vacuum bag on the workshop table">
</figure>

I learned how to do a carbon fiber layup with vacuum bag sealing and used it to make a custom fuselage. The fuselage was formed using 3D printed molds, and each layup was built up on the mold one layer at a time.

I painted epoxy onto each layer of carbon fiber to seal it. As I stacked the layers, I rotated each one by 90 degrees from the one before it. Carbon fiber weave can leave small gaps, and alternating the direction of the fibers closes them and makes the finished part stronger in more than one direction.

Once the carbon fiber layers were down, I built the rest of the stack on top of them, following a standard vacuum bagging layup. The order, from the part outward, was:

1. **Peel ply**, which is removed after curing and leaves a clean surface behind.
2. **Release film**, which keeps the layers from sticking to what is on top of them.
3. **Breather ply**, which gives air a path to escape evenly across the whole part.

I then sealed the whole stack inside a vacuum bag and pulled the air out with a vacuum pump. With the air removed, the pressure of the atmosphere pushes the layers tightly against the mold and squeezes out extra epoxy and trapped air, which gives a denser, more consistent part.

### Design Report Cover

<figure class="side-image">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/coverdesign24.jpeg' | relative_url }}" alt="Cover design for the Cornell Design Build Fly 2024-2025 design report">
</figure>

While writing my detailed report sections on landing gear testing and glider fabrication/testing, I also designed the cover for the report. I wanted our cover to be reminiscent of Tucson, Arizona, which was the location of the 2025 AIAA DBF Fly Off. I created the cover in Goodnotes and used Google Images to inform the mountain and desert landscape. The plane pictured is our actual SolidWorks assembly.
